# Public Brand Name Validation

Date: 2026-09-20
Status: ACTIVE VALIDATION — NOT YET FROZEN

## Standard

NINFA should not freeze a public channel name merely because no large YouTube channel uses it. A candidate must avoid meaningful collision with active brands, products, channels, publications, and same-category commercial phrases.

Search absence is not equivalent to legal clearance. Final freeze still requires direct handle claiming/availability checks and, if the brand becomes commercially material, formal trademark clearance in the jurisdictions where protection is desired.

## Rejected candidates

### Actually Automated — REJECT

Reason: no dominant exact-name channel was found, but Kortical currently uses the exact phrase “Actually automated” as part of the commercial claim for its AI Agents product (“Real work. Actually automated.”). Under NINFA's strict uniqueness standard this is too close to our intended category.

Reference: https://kortical.com/

### AI Under Load — REJECT

Reason: Sparq already publishes and actively promotes a white paper titled **AI Under Load** about AI operating in production systems. The phrase is already associated with a technology company and substantially overlaps NINFA's intended editorial territory.

Reference: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/teamsparq_our-newest-white-paper-ai-under-load-examines-activity-7445149232325451776-vRx5

### Execora — REJECT

Reason: multiple active businesses/products already use the exact name, including Execora Consulting, execora.net, execora.co, and execora.space.

References:
- https://www.execora.net/
- https://www.execora.co/
- https://www.execora.space/

## Current leading candidate

# Does It Automate?

Handle form: `DoesItAutomate`

### Indexed collision screen

As of 2026-09-20, broad and exact-match searches did **not** identify an active company, YouTube channel, software product, podcast, newsletter, GitHub project, or indexed trademark result using **Does It Automate?** / **DoesItAutomate** as its exact brand identity.

The words do appear descriptively inside articles and product discussions (for example questions such as “does it automate tests?”). That is acceptable because the phrase is being used descriptively, not as a competing identity.

### Why it fits NINFA

- It is a memorable editorial question.
- Every video can answer it with evidence.
- It supports builds, experiments, comparisons, business economics, and stress tests.
- It does not lock the channel to one AI vendor or tool category.
- It can support aggressive YouTube packaging while keeping a consistent brand promise.

Working brand promise:

> **We test AI systems on real work.**

Alternative:

> **AI systems. Real work. Does it automate?**

### Remaining checks before freeze

1. Directly confirm and claim the YouTube handle `@DoesItAutomate`.
2. Check/claim preferred social handles on X, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn and other channels we actually intend to operate.
3. Confirm registrar-level availability of preferred domains (`doesitautomate.com`, `.ai`, `.io` or acceptable alternative). Search-engine absence alone does not prove domain availability.
4. Run a direct trademark database clearance for the intended media/education/software-adjacent categories if we decide to invest materially in the brand.
5. Freeze only after handles/domain strategy is resolved.

## Decision

`Does It Automate?` currently passes NINFA's **public indexed-collision screen**, but remains **PROVISIONAL** until direct handle/domain availability is confirmed.
