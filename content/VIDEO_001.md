## Promise

The video launches the experiment honestly. It does **not** claim that the channel is already profitable or fully autonomous.

The viewer should understand:
- what NINFA is trying to automate,
- what tools/repositories are being used,
- what remains human,
- the initial operating budget,
- how success/failure will be measured,
- why the experiment is commercially interesting,
- what will be reported publicly later.

## Target duration

10–13 minutes.

## Narrative structure

### 0:00–0:25 — Cold open

Most people selling “automated YouTube” show the finished screenshots.

We are starting at zero.

This channel has almost no audience, no revenue, and one job:

**find out whether AI can actually operate a serious YouTube media business.**

### 0:25–1:20 — The challenge

Define the rules:
- English-language channel.
- AI/software/automation niche.
- Long-form first.
- Real research, demos, and measurable outcomes.
- 90–95% production automation target.
- Human remains the final editorial gate.
- Initial capital is available, but spending is evidence-driven.

No fake revenue. No bought audience. No fabricated experiments.

### 1:20–2:20 — Why this niche

Explain the hypothesis:
- AI/software/automation has strong commercial intent.
- SaaS affiliates create monetization before AdSense.
- Real builds/tests are more defensible than generic AI-news content.
- The market changes fast enough to create recurring subjects.

Do not quote unverified RPM or earnings as guarantees.

### 2:20–4:20 — The machine

Visualize:

Market signals
→ topic discovery
→ scoring
→ research
→ real experiment
→ script
→ fact-check
→ software capture
→ narration/assets
→ automated edit
→ thumbnail/title
→ human QA
→ publish
→ analytics
→ learning loop

Show Project NINFA architecture and the actual repo.

### 4:20–5:45 — What we're reusing

Introduce:
- MoneyPrinterTurbo
- TubeAssistant
- youtube-automation-agent

Explain that NINFA will reuse commodity media plumbing but build its own intelligence/research/learning layers.

Be explicit that these projects are reference/foundation candidates and are not all equally mature.

### 5:45–7:00 — The economics

Show the current rules:
- budget is available, not mandatory spend,
- measure cost per video,
- AI video is used selectively,
- revenue can come from affiliates before ads,
- scale only when quality/data justify it.

Do **not** invent a current cost-per-video yet.

### 7:00–8:20 — What remains human

Human editor-in-chief:
- approves major topics,
- approves final factual claims,
- final QA,
- occasional on-camera presence.

Everything else is a candidate for automation.

### 8:20–9:30 — Success and failure criteria

Track:
- impressions,
- CTR,
- first-30-second retention,
- average view duration,
- watch hours,
- subscribers,
- audience geography,
- affiliate clicks/conversions,
- production cost,
- revenue.

Milestones:
first 1,000 views → first 100 subscribers → first $1 → first $100 → break-even → positive unit economics.

### 9:30–10:30 — The experiment begins

Reveal:
- channel brand: Does It Automate?
- the operating promise: We test AI systems on real work.
- this channel itself is experiment #1.

### 10:30–end — CTA

> Subscribe if you want to see the numbers, failures, and actual system evolve. If this works, you'll see exactly how. If it fails, you'll see that too.
