# Brand Identity — Does It Automate?

Status: **FROZEN v1**
Public brand: **Does It Automate?**
Handle: **@DoesItAutomate**
Internal engine: **Project NINFA**

## Brand promise

> **We test AI systems on real work.**

Supporting lines:
- **AI systems. Real work. Real results.**
- **BUILD · TEST · AUTOMATE**
- Brand-guide line: **Test. Automate. Real Results.**

## Positioning

Does It Automate? is an independent AI/software experiment channel that builds systems, tests them on real work, measures results and shows failures instead of hiding them.

The channel should feel like:
- engineering lab,
- experiment dashboard,
- modern technology media,
- skeptical of hype,
- outcome-driven,
- technically credible but accessible.

## Audience

Primary:
- developers,
- technical professionals,
- solopreneurs,
- freelancers,
- automation builders,
- agencies,
- technically curious business users.

Primary commercial geography:
- United States,
- Canada,
- United Kingdom,
- Australia,
- other English-speaking high-value markets.

## Canonical logo rule

The **canonical mark is the blue monogram / upward-execution-arrow identity shown in the latest approved Does It Automate? brand guide supplied by the owner**.

Critical rule:
- use the exact approved asset,
- preserve its proportions,
- preserve the approved blue treatment,
- do not redraw it,
- do not approximate it,
- do not recolor it,
- do not substitute an older mark.

## Visual system

Base:
- graphite / near-black backgrounds,
- off-white typography,
- electric blue / cyan energy accent,
- high contrast,
- clean technical layouts.

Prefer:
- real interfaces,
- browser recordings,
- code,
- logs,
- numbers,
- charts,
- diagrams,
- annotations,
- experiment states,
- failure states.

Avoid:
- generic purple AI gradients,
- robot-head clichés,
- glowing-brain imagery,
- decorative synthetic B-roll with no information value.

## Logo usage

Use the logo intentionally:
1. intro brand beat,
2. small restrained watermark during normal content,
3. section transitions when useful,
4. small thumbnail corner mark,
5. outro / subscribe card.

The logo should not become the main thumbnail subject unless the episode is explicitly about the brand.

## Thumbnail doctrine

**Maximum curiosity without breaking the promise.**

Rules:
- one dominant visual contrast,
- short text, usually 0–4 words,
- real evidence over generic AI imagery,
- title and thumbnail should complement rather than duplicate each other,
- measured results may appear only after they actually exist.

Examples:
- `PAID vs $0`
- `FAILED 37%`
- `100 TASKS`
- `NOT READY`
- `IT WORKED`

## Editorial voice

- curious,
- technical,
- skeptical,
- direct,
- entertaining,
- comfortable showing failure,
- transparent about cost and limitations.

## First-five-seconds doctrine

Open aggressively when the experiment supports it, but pay off the promise immediately with real evidence.

Clickbait is acceptable only when the video substantively fulfills it.

## Brand architecture

- **Does It Automate?** = public media brand.
- **NINFA** = internal autonomous media engine / production system.
- NINFA may appear in behind-the-scenes or architecture content but should not replace the public brand.
