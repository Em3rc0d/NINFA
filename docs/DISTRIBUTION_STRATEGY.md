# Distribution Strategy

Status: **ACTIVE v1**

## Objective

Do not treat YouTube as “upload MP4 and hope.”

Every long-form experiment should become a small traffic system.

## Growth loop

Packaging → distribution → retention → satisfaction → analytics → next-video improvement.

## Per-episode asset model

For each strong long-form episode, create:
1. **Long-form YouTube video** — primary product.
2. **2–3 Shorts** — strongest failure, result, comparison or counterintuitive moment.
3. **1 image/carousel/community post** — compact evidence summary.
4. **1 poll/teaser** — involve the audience in the next test.
5. **End screen** — point to the most relevant adjacent episode.
6. **Card** — only when contextually relevant.
7. **Pinned comment** — ask an easy, specific question and tease the next experiment.

## Packaging

For important long-form uploads:
- generate multiple title hypotheses,
- generate multiple materially different thumbnail concepts,
- use YouTube A/B testing when available,
- avoid changing packaging reactively before enough impressions exist.

## Diagnostic framework

### Low impressions
Likely distribution/history/topic-fit problem.

### Impressions + low CTR
Packaging problem: title/thumbnail.

### Clicks + early drop
Hook/promise mismatch.

### Strong first 30 seconds + later drop
Pacing/story/value-density problem.

### Strong retention + poor next-video flow
Internal-linking / channel-sequencing problem.

### Shorts get views but long-form does not
The Short-to-long bridge is weak.

## First-30-seconds rule

The opening must rapidly:
- confirm the title/thumbnail promise,
- show real evidence,
- establish stakes,
- avoid generic channel intros.

Aggressive hooks are encouraged when truthful.

## Topic clustering

Current sequence:
1. local AI video factory,
2. paid vs local video workflow,
3. AI agent × 100 real tasks,
4. n8n vs AI agents,
5. low-cost AI employee,
6. automations businesses may actually pay for.

## Learning policy

Do not judge the channel from two uploads.

Accumulate a coherent sample and enough impressions before making large strategic pivots.
